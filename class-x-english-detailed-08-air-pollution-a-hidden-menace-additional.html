⭐ Additional Questions & Answers

Extra practice questions prepared from the chapter content.

Q1. How can air pollution affect buildings and monuments?
Answer: Pollutants and acid rain can damage stone, marble and other materials.
Q2. Why are children especially mentioned in relation to acid rain?
Answer: The lesson notes irritation to sensitive tissues such as the eyes and lungs, particularly in children.
Q3. What is the difference between preventive and collection measures?
Answer: Preventive measures reduce pollution at its source; collection measures trap pollutants before release.
Q4. Why are smokestacks raised?
Answer: Raising them is described as a dispersal measure.
Q5. What is the significance of the Bhopal reference?
Answer: It introduces the serious consequences associated with industrial pollution.
Q6. Why does controlling pollution cost money?
Answer: Pollution-control equipment, research and new technologies require expenditure.
Q7. What is a catalytic converter?
Answer: It is a device mentioned in the lesson that changes pollutants in car exhaust into less harmful substances.
Q8. Why is international cooperation relevant?
Answer: Air pollutants can travel across national borders.
Q9. What is one practical school-level response to the lesson?
Answer: Students can observe pollution in their locality and prepare a project on causes, effects and control.
Q10. What is the central message?
Answer: Clean air is essential and controlling pollution requires sustained individual, technological and governmental action.
