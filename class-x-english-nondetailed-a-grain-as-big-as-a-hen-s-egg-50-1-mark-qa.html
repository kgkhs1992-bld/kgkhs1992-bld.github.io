A Grain as Big as a Hen's Egg

Class X English • Non-Detailed Text • 50 One-Mark Questions & Answers

← Back to Class X English
1. Who wrote 'A Grain as Big as a Hen's Egg'?
Answer: Leo Tolstoy.
2. What did the children find?
Answer: A grain shaped like corn and as large as a hen's egg.
3. Where did the children find the object?
Answer: In a ravine.
4. What did the object look like?
Answer: A large grain of corn.
5. Who bought the object from the children?
Answer: A traveller.
6. How much did the traveller pay the children?
Answer: A penny.
7. To whom did the traveller sell the object?
Answer: The king.
8. Why did the traveller sell it to the king?
Answer: As a curiosity.
9. Who did the king ask to identify it?
Answer: His wise men.
10. How did the wise men discover what it was?
Answer: A hen pecked a hole in it.
11. What did the hen reveal?
Answer: That it was a grain of corn.
12. What did the king then ask his learned men?
Answer: When and where such corn had grown.
13. Where did the learned men search for an answer?
Answer: In their books.
14. Could the learned men find the answer in their books?
Answer: No.
15. Whom did they suggest the king ask?
Answer: Old peasants.
16. What kind of peasant was first brought to the king?
Answer: A very old, bent and toothless man.
17. What aids did the old peasant use?
Answer: Two crutches.
18. Could the first old man identify the grain as his own crop?
Answer: No.
19. What did the first old man say about corn in his father's time?
Answer: It had been larger and yielded more flour.
20. Who was brought before the king next?
Answer: The first old man's father.
21. How did the second old man walk?
Answer: He walked without crutches.
22. How were the second old man's eyes?
Answer: Clear.
23. How was his hearing?
Answer: Good.
24. Did the second old man recognize the grain?
Answer: He recognized it as the same kind.
25. Where did corn like that grow in his youth?
Answer: Everywhere.
26. Did people buy and sell bread in his time?
Answer: No.
27. What did people do instead of buying bread?
Answer: They grew their own corn.
28. Who did the second old man say owned the land?
Answer: God's earth.
29. What did he say was man's own possession?
Answer: Labour.
30. What had changed in later times?
Answer: People had begun depending on others' labour.
31. What happened to the size of the grain over time?
Answer: It became smaller.
32. What happened to the health of the later generations?
Answer: They became weaker.
33. Why was the grandfather healthier?
Answer: He lived by his own labour.
34. What did the grandfather say about coveting others' produce?
Answer: People in his time did not covet what others produced.
35. What principle did people follow in the old time?
Answer: God's law.
36. What was the main source of food in the story?
Answer: Corn.
37. What did the king want to learn from the grandfather?
Answer: Why the grain had once been larger.
38. What two physical differences did the king notice among generations?
Answer: Their strength and ability to walk.
39. What did the grandfather say about money?
Answer: People did not know or use it in his time.
40. What did people share with one another?
Answer: Their produce when necessary.
41. What did the first old man say about his own generation's grain?
Answer: It was larger and yielded more flour than present-day grain.
42. What did the grandfather do with the grain?
Answer: He tasted it.
43. What did the grandfather say about the grain?
Answer: It was the very same kind as the old grain.
44. What did the grandfather say about land?
Answer: It was free and not privately owned.
45. What did people call their own?
Answer: Their labour.
46. What caused the decline in grain size?
Answer: People stopped living by their own labour.
47. What did people begin depending on?
Answer: The labour of others.
48. What is a major theme of the story?
Answer: The value of honest labour and simple living.
49. Who is the author?
Answer: Leo Tolstoy.
50. What does the title refer to?
Answer: The unusually large grain found by the children.
