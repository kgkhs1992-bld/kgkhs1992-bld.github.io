⭐ Additional Questions & Answers

Extra practice questions prepared from the chapter content.

Q1. How does the poem use shock to communicate a message?
Answer: The image of a brain in a jar immediately makes the consequences of accidents concrete and memorable.
Q2. Why should road safety be treated as a shared responsibility?
Answer: Drivers, riders, passengers and pedestrians all influence safety on roads.
Q3. How can speed increase risk?
Answer: Higher speed can reduce reaction time and increase the severity of a collision.
Q4. Why is alcohol dangerous for drivers?
Answer: It can impair judgement, coordination and reaction.
Q5. How can mobile-phone use distract a driver?
Answer: It takes attention away from driving and reduces awareness of traffic.
Q6. Why is visibility important?
Answer: Drivers and pedestrians need to see hazards and be seen by others.
Q7. What is the connection between the title and the message?
Answer: The title makes the reader confront a possible consequence of unsafe road behaviour.
Q8. What attitude toward road rules does the lesson encourage?
Answer: It encourages awareness and responsible observance of safety practices.
Q9. What is the broader educational value of the poem?
Answer: It combines literary imagination with a practical message about protecting human life.
Q10. What should a reader remember after studying the poem?
Answer: Safe road behaviour can help reduce avoidable risk and injury.
