📖 Chapter at a Glance

Author / Poet: Mahatma Gandhi
Chapter: At the High School
Textbook page: 28

Gandhi recalls his experiences as a high-school student. He describes his studies, teachers, scholarships, character, sports, handwriting and the incident when he was wrongly suspected of being absent from gymnastics. He also recalls the rivalry between his Sanskrit and Persian teachers. The lesson shows how his ideas about physical training and handwriting changed with experience.

About the Author

The lesson is an autobiographical account by Mahatma Gandhi. The textbook presents his school years and his reflections on his own conduct, teachers and education.

Gandhi as a Student

Gandhi says that he was not regarded as a fool and enjoyed the affection of his teachers. Certificates of progress and character were sent to his parents. He won prizes and obtained scholarships in the fifth and sixth standards.

Sports and Discipline

Gymnastics and cricket were made compulsory for upper-standard boys. Gandhi disliked them partly because of shyness and because he wanted to serve his father. Later he recognized that physical training deserves an important place along with mental training.

Sanskrit and Persian

Gandhi was drawn toward Persian because it seemed easier. His Sanskrit teacher, Krishnashankar Pandya, encouraged him to return to Sanskrit, stressing its importance as the language of his cultural tradition. The lesson describes a rivalry between the Sanskrit and Persian teachers.

Handwriting and Character

Gandhi later realized that good handwriting has value. The lesson also emphasizes his careful concern for character and his strong emotional reaction to blame or any blemish on his conduct.

Important Glossary

affection — fondness; merit — ability or talent; scholarship — financial award for study; blemish — moral defect; jealously — very carefully; disciplinarian — one who maintains discipline; rivalry — competition.
