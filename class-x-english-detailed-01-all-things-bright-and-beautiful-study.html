📖 Chapter at a Glance

Author / Poet: C. F. Alexander
Chapter: All Things Bright and Beautiful
Textbook page: 01

The poem praises the beauty, variety and order of God’s creation. It describes flowers, birds, mountains, rivers, sunrise and sunset, the seasons, fruit, trees, meadows and waterside plants. The poet finally reminds us that human beings have eyes to see creation and lips to speak about its greatness.

About the Poet

Cecil Frances Alexander was born in Dublin, Ireland, in 1818. She was a famous hymn writer and poet. The poem is included in Hymn for Children.

Key Ideas

• Beauty and variety in nature
• Gratitude for creation
• The relationship between the Creator and the created world
• Human senses as gifts that help us appreciate nature

Stanza-wise Study

Stanza 1: All things and creatures are described as bright, beautiful, wise and wonderful.
Stanza 2: Flowers and birds are presented through their colours, songs and wings.
Stanza 3: The mountain, river, sunset and morning sky are described.
Stanza 4: Winter wind, summer sun and ripe garden fruit are mentioned.
Stanza 5: Trees, meadows and rushes form another part of the natural world.
Stanza 6: The poet concludes by referring to eyes and lips as gifts for seeing and praising creation.

Important Glossary

bright — shining; creatures — living beings; glowing — bright; meadow — open grassland; rushes — waterside plants; Almighty — all-powerful.

Textbook Focus

1. What does the poet think of all things?
2. Who has made all these things?
3. How does the poet describe flowers and birds?
4. What is meant by the purple-headed mountain?
5. Why are the meadows useful?
6. Why has God given us eyes and lips?

Revision Points

Remember the natural objects in each stanza, the two seasons mentioned, the meaning of “purple-headed”, and the poem’s final expression of gratitude.
