Book Q & A

Original KGK answers based on the Class IX textbook chapter.

1. Who is the poet?
Answer: William Cowper.
2. Who does “I” refer to?
Answer: Alexander Selkirk.
3. Who is the monarch in the poem?
Answer: Selkirk, because he is alone on the island and surveys it as his domain.
4. What is solitude?
Answer: Being without companions; loneliness.
5. Why are the beasts unfamiliar with Selkirk?
Answer: They are unacquainted with human beings.
6. What does Selkirk ask the wind to do?
Answer: Carry news of his friends and native land.
7. What makes him despair?
Answer: His memory of home followed by the realization that he is still isolated.
8. What does he miss?
Answer: His native land, friends and human companionship.
9. What does the tempest symbolize in comparison with thought?
Answer: Even a storm seems slow compared with the speed of the mind's movement.
10. What happens when evening comes?
Answer: The sea bird returns to its nest, the beast to its lair, and Selkirk returns to his cabin.
11. What does sea fowl mean?
Answer: A sea bird.
12. What does lair mean?
Answer: A wild animal's resting place or den.
13. What does affliction mean?
Answer: Suffering or distress.
14. What does Selkirk call himself in his isolation?
Answer: The monarch of all he surveys.
15. What does the poem reveal about human companionship?
Answer: It is deeply valued and cannot easily be replaced by possessions or surroundings.
16. Why does Selkirk remember his friends?
Answer: He longs for contact and reassurance that they still think of him.
17. What is the setting?
Answer: A lonely, desolate island.
18. What does the poem show about freedom?
Answer: Physical freedom can coexist with emotional loneliness.
19. Why does Selkirk repair to his cabin?
Answer: The day is ending and the natural world is settling into rest.
20. What is the main message?
Answer: Human companionship, home and emotional connection are essential to a person's well-being.
