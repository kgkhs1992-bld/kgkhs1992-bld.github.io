📝 Class IX Geometry — Chapter 05

MENSURATION — Study Material

Chapter 5 deals with finding areas of plane figures and applying the results to triangles, rhombi, trapezia and quadrilaterals.

5.1 Introduction

Important terms used in mensuration include base, height, hypotenuse and perpendicular. For a right-angled triangle, the hypotenuse is the side opposite the right angle.

For a right triangle: h² = p² + b²

5.2 Polygonal Region and its Area

The area of a polygonal region is measured in square units. The textbook develops area results from the basic area postulates and familiar plane figures.

Rectangle = length × breadth
Parallelogram = base × corresponding height
Triangle = ½ × base × corresponding height

5.3 Heron's Formula

For a triangle with sides a, b, c, first find the semi-perimeter:

s = (a + b + c) / 2

Then its area is:

Area = √[s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)]

The chapter applies this formula to triangles and to quadrilaterals by dividing a quadrilateral into two triangles.

5.4 Rhombus

A rhombus is a quadrilateral with all four sides equal. If its diagonals are d₁ and d₂, the area is:

Area of rhombus = ½ × d₁ × d₂

The textbook also derives the relation using the four right triangles formed by the diagonals.

5.5 Trapezium

A trapezium has one pair of parallel sides. If the parallel sides are a and b, and the perpendicular height is h:

Area = ½(a + b)h

The chapter derives this by splitting the trapezium into triangles.

5.6 Area of a Quadrilateral

A quadrilateral can be divided into two triangles by a diagonal. The total area is the sum of the areas of the two triangles.

Area of quadrilateral = Area of first triangle + Area of second triangle

Heron's formula can be used for the two triangles when their three side lengths are known.
