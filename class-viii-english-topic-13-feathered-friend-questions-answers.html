Science and Curiosity: Feathered Friend

Class VIII – Jasmine English | Questions & Answers

1. What can be inferred about Sven from the description of his work?
Sven is a skilled and capable construction worker. His work requires him to handle floating girders accurately and assemble them in the difficult conditions of space.
2. How can we conclude that Sven was working in zero gravity?
The girders are described as floating and being moved in a slow, three-dimensional way. The crew also works in the absence of ordinary gravity, which indicates a weightless environment.
3. Why did the narrator think Sven had sneaked a bird aboard? What was Sven's scientific reason?
Sven had secretly brought Claribel because he wanted to observe how a bird would operate in conditions where it had no weight but could still use its wings. He described this as scientific curiosity.
4. How did Claribel adjust to the new surroundings?
Claribel adapted remarkably well. She could remain suspended in the air, perform unusual movements and move around the station without difficulty. She also became comfortable enough to behave as a pet.
5. What made the narrator mistake the musical whistle for a sound from the intercom?
The narrator was sitting in his small office when he heard a musical whistle beside his ear. Because he was in a space station and expected communication through the intercom, he first assumed the sound came from it.
6. Why did the crew initially think that Claribel had died?
Claribel was found limp and apparently unconscious, with her claws clenched. Jock could not detect a heartbeat, although he admitted that he had never listened to a canary's heart.
7. How was Claribel revived the first time?
The crew placed her in an oxygen mask. She revived immediately and briefly became active again.
8. What made the narrator realise that something was wrong with the air?
Claribel revived with oxygen but collapsed again. This reminded the narrator that miners had used canaries to warn them about dangerous gases. He therefore suspected that the station's air was unsafe.
9. Why did Jim initially dismiss the warning?
Jim believed that the duplicate alarm circuits would have detected an air problem and sounded an alarm. He did not initially know that the second circuit was not yet connected.
10. What had caused the alarm system to fail?
A rare eclipse by Earth's shadow caused part of the air purifier to freeze. The single connected alarm then failed to go off, while the second alarm circuit had not yet been connected.
11. What does the phrase “shook Jim” mean in the story?
It means that the unexpected information startled Jim and made him realise that the situation could be serious.
12. What does the crew's passing the oxygen bottle “like a pipe of peace” suggest?
It suggests that the crew was united and cooperating in an emergency. They shared the oxygen while trying to protect Claribel and themselves.
13. How does the crew's attitude towards Claribel change?
At first Claribel is a secret pet whose presence must be concealed from visitors. Later the whole crew accepts her. When she collapses, they become worried and try to save her. Finally they realise that she has helped save them by warning them about the air.
14. Why is the title “Feathered Friend” appropriate?
Claribel is a feathered bird who becomes a companion to the crew. She also becomes an unexpected helper because her reaction to the air problem warns the crew of danger.
15. What does “Without Claribel, we should soon have been slightly dead” suggest?
It humorously emphasises the seriousness of the air problem. Without Claribel's warning, the crew might not have discovered the danger in time.
16. How does the writer combine science fiction with humour?
The story uses a futuristic space station, weightlessness, air systems and engineering problems as its science-fiction setting. Humour comes from secretly keeping a canary in space, Claribel's unusual movements and the crew's playful reactions to the situation.
17. What lesson about problem-solving does the story give?
It shows that useful clues can come from unexpected sources. Careful observation, memory, scientific reasoning and willingness to reconsider an assumption can help solve a problem.
18. What might Sven reflect on after the incident?
Sven might feel grateful that Claribel survived and relieved that her presence helped protect the crew. He may also recognise that his curiosity had an unexpected practical value.
19. Who was Salim Ali?
Salim Ali was an Indian ornithologist, popularly known as the “Birdman of India”. The textbook says that he conducted systematic bird surveys across India and wrote several books on birds. It also records his Padma Bhushan and Padma Vibhushan awards.
20. What is meant by “a canary in the coal mine”?
It is a phrase used for an early warning of possible danger. The lesson explains that canaries were used by miners because the birds were sensitive to dangerous gases.
