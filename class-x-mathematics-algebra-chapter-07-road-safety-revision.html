📐 2. Arithmetic Progression – Formula Box

n-th term: an = a + (n − 1)d
Last term: l = a + (n − 1)d
Sum of n terms: Sn = n/2 [2a + (n − 1)d]
Also: Sn = n/2 (a + l)
Mini Example:
For 5, 8, 11, 14, ... the common difference is d = 8 − 5 = 3.
Exam tip: Before applying a formula, write down a, d, n and, if known, l.

📊 3. Percentage & Data Revision

Percentage = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100
Percentage increase = (Increase ÷ Original value) × 100
Percentage decrease = (Decrease ÷ Original value) × 100
Question typeWhat to identify first
Percentage of studentsPart and total number of students
Increase in a quantityOriginal value and increase
Decrease in a quantityOriginal value and decrease
Bar graphScale, labels and units
Common trap: Do not divide by the changed value when the question asks for percentage increase/decrease relative to the original value.

🥧 4. Pie Chart – Quick Revision

Full circle = 360°
Sector angle = (Part ÷ Total) × 360°
Part = (Sector angle ÷ 360°) × Total
Mini Example:
If a category represents one-fourth of the total, its sector angle is 360° ÷ 4 = 90°.
  • Add all sector angles to check that they represent the complete circle.
  • Read the legend/category labels carefully.
  • Do not confuse a percentage with a sector angle.

📐 5. Trigonometry – Essential Ratios

sin θ = Perpendicular ÷ Hypotenuse
cos θ = Base ÷ Hypotenuse
tan θ = Perpendicular ÷ Base
RatioUse
sin θOpposite/Perpendicular and Hypotenuse
cos θBase/Adjacent and Hypotenuse
tan θPerpendicular and Base/Adjacent
Remember: First mark the given angle. Then identify the perpendicular, base and hypotenuse relative to that angle.

📏 6. Pythagoras & Road-Safety Geometry

Hypotenuse² = Perpendicular² + Base²
Use: In a right-angled road-safety diagram, if two sides are known, use Pythagoras to find the third side where applicable.
  • The hypotenuse is always opposite the right angle.
  • The hypotenuse is the longest side of a right-angled triangle.
  • Do not use Pythagoras unless the relevant triangle is right-angled.

🚗 7. Reaction, Braking & Stopping Distance

Reaction Distance

Distance travelled while the driver reacts to a hazard before braking begins.

Braking Distance

Distance travelled after braking is applied until the vehicle stops.

Stopping Distance

The total distance needed to stop, combining the relevant reaction and braking portions.

Stopping distance = Reaction distance + Braking distance
Important: Keep these three terms separate when reading a road-safety problem. Use exactly the quantities given in the question.

⏱️ 8. Speed & Unit Conversion

Speed = Distance ÷ Time
Distance = Speed × Time
Time = Distance ÷ Speed
ConversionRule
km/h → m/sMultiply by 5/18
m/s → km/hMultiply by 18/5
Check: Make the units compatible before substituting values into a formula.

📹 9. CCTV / Height & Distance Problems

  • Draw a simple right-angled triangle before calculating.
  • Mark the camera/observer height, horizontal distance and line of sight.
  • Identify the given angle carefully.
  • Select sin, cos or tan according to the sides involved.
  • If the question asks for height or distance, write the final answer with its unit.
Typical setup:
If the angle and horizontal distance are known and the required quantity is the perpendicular height, tan θ is often the relevant ratio.
