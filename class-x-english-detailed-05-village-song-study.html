📖 Chapter at a Glance

Author / Poet: Sarojini Naidu
Chapter: Village Song
Textbook page: 41

The poem is written as a conversation between a mother and her daughter. The mother asks the “honey child” where she is going and reminds her of marriage, bridal songs, bridal clothes and the pleasures of family life. The daughter is attracted instead by the wild forest, the river-isles, flowers and the voices of the fairy folk. The poem contrasts domestic expectations with the child’s longing for nature and freedom.

About the Poet

Sarojini Naidu (1879–1949) was a poet, freedom fighter and public figure. The textbook notes that she was popularly known as the Nightingale of India and that “Village Song” appeared in The Golden Threshold.

About the Poem

The poem draws on the tradition of folk poetry, with simple language, everyday imagery and the joys and sorrows of rural life. It presents the voices of a mother and daughter.

The Mother’s Voice

The mother lovingly calls the girl “honey child”. She refers to golden grain, bridal songs, bridal robes and bridal cakes and asks her daughter to remain for the expected marriage.

The Daughter’s Voice

The daughter wants to go to the wild forest, where champa flowers bloom, the koil sings and the fairy folk seem to call her. She finds the forest sounds sweeter than the worldly celebrations described by her mother.

Important Glossary

honey child — affectionate address; breeze — wind; champa — flower; koil — song-bird; bridal songs — marriage songs; cradle songs — lullabies; robes — clothes; cadence — rhythm; forest notes — sounds of nature.

Textbook Focus

1. Who are the two speakers?
2. Where does the honey child want to go?
3. What does the mother say about the world of pleasure?
4. Why does the daughter prefer the forest?
5. What do “cadences of sorrow” and “wind of death” suggest?

Poetic Features

The poem uses dialogue, repetition, natural imagery and contrast. Bridal and cradle songs are associated with changing human life, while forest music represents freedom and attraction to nature.

Revision Points

Remember the two voices, the forest images, the mother’s marriage imagery and the daughter’s attraction to nature.
