Questions & Answers

Class VIII • Jasmine English • Topic 15
Bibha Chowdhuri: The Beam of Light that Lit the Path for Women in Indian Science

Let Us Discuss

1. Arrange these events in the correct order: IAU renamed a star “Bibha”; she became the first woman faculty member at TIFR; she was born; Government of India declared a chair professorship; she joined Manchester under Patrick M. S. Blackett; she was nominated for a Nobel Prize.

Answer: 1) Born in 1913 → 2) Joined the University of Manchester in 1945 → 3) Became the first woman faculty member at TIFR in 1949 → 4) Nominated for a Nobel Prize in 1950 → 5) IAU renamed the star HD 86081 “Bibha” in 2019 → 6) Government of India declared a chair professorship in her name in 2020.

Let Us Think and Reflect

2. Why is the mention of Patrick M. S. Blackett significant?

It shows that Bibha studied cosmic rays under the guidance of a celebrated Nobel Laureate at the University of Manchester. This was an important stage in her academic and research journey.

3. What does the newspaper title “India’s New Woman Scientist—She has an eye for Cosmic Rays” suggest about public perception in the 1940s?

It suggests that women scientists were still viewed with doubt or as unusual in a scientific world largely associated with men.

4. In “celebrated Nobel Laureate”, what does “celebrated” mean?

It means famous, highly respected and well regarded for achievements.

5. Complete: “She has an eye for Cosmic Rays” suggests that…

she had a strong interest in, ability for, and scientific understanding of cosmic rays.

6. What can be inferred about the challenges faced by women scientists today compared with Bibha Chowdhuri’s era?

The lesson presents progress but says challenges still remain. Women have greater participation and leadership opportunities in science and technology today, while the earlier period involved stronger social and institutional barriers.

7. What does “Bibha Chowdhuri’s legacy lives on” mean?

Her work, example and influence continue to inspire later generations, especially women pursuing science.

8. Why is Bibha Chowdhuri called a “beacon” and a “beam of light”?

The expressions symbolise guidance and inspiration. Her pioneering example is presented as a light that helps guide women who follow careers in science.

9. Is the statement “Bibha Chowdhuri was the beacon, the ‘beam of light’ whose story continues to inspire young women” a fact or an opinion?

In the exercise's context, it is an opinion/interpretive statement because “beacon” and “beam of light” are figurative descriptions of her inspirational role.

Answer the Following Questions

10. Why is Bibha Chowdhuri considered a pioneer in science?

She entered a male-dominated scientific field at a time when women had fewer opportunities, became an early Indian woman physicist, pursued advanced research in cosmic rays and subatomic particles, and became the first woman faculty member at TIFR.

11. What does her Nobel nomination by Erwin Schrödinger tell us about her abilities?

It indicates that her scientific work was regarded as significant enough for her to receive a Nobel Prize nomination from the noted physicist Erwin Schrödinger, even though the nomination did not result in the prize.

12. Bibha Chowdhuri never worked for awards or recognition. Support this statement.

The lesson says she continued her work tirelessly despite not receiving awards during her lifetime. Her research continued through institutions including TIFR, the Physical Research Laboratory and the Saha Institute of Nuclear Physics.

13. How does renaming the star “Bibha” serve as recognition?

It is a literal tribute because a star was given her name, and a symbolic tribute because “Bibha” is explained in the lesson as meaning “beam of light”, matching the lesson's image of her as a guiding light in science.

14. How do women in ISRO today reflect changes in attitudes towards women in STEM?

The lesson presents women as participants and leaders in major space missions. It contrasts this broader participation with the barriers faced by Bibha Chowdhuri's generation.

15. What is the writer’s purpose in highlighting Bibha Chowdhuri and modern women scientists?

The lesson aims to present the journey of women in Indian science, remember an early pioneer, and show how later women scientists have continued and expanded that journey.

16. How might the text shape perceptions of women in traditionally male-dominated fields?

By presenting a documented scientific career and examples of women working in science and space research, the text can encourage readers to recognise women's ability to contribute to fields once dominated by men.

Long Answer Practice

28. Explain how the title “The Beam of Light that Lit the Path for Women in Indian Science” suits the lesson.

The title combines the literal meaning associated with “Bibha” and the figurative idea of guidance. Bibha Chowdhuri is presented as an early woman physicist who continued scientific work despite barriers. Her example helped form a path that later women in Indian science and space research could follow. Therefore, the “beam of light” represents both her name and her inspirational legacy.

29. Explain the main contrast between Bibha Chowdhuri’s era and the present day as presented in the lesson.

The lesson describes Bibha's era as a time when women faced stronger barriers to education, recognition and scientific careers. It then describes greater participation and leadership by women in present-day Indian STEM and space missions. The text also states that the journey is not complete and challenges remain.
