📖 Chapter at a Glance

Author / Poet: G. L. Fuentes
Chapter: A Letter to God
Textbook page: 08

Lencho, a farmer, hopes for rain because his ripe cornfield needs water. Rain arrives, but a violent hailstorm destroys the crop. Lencho has complete faith in God and writes a letter asking for money. The postmaster and his employees collect money and send it to him. Lencho receives the money but believes that God sent it and suspects the post-office employees of keeping the rest.

About the Author

The textbook identifies G. L. Fuentes as the writer of the story.

Story Flow

Beginning: Lencho watches the sky and expects rain.
Storm: Rain changes into a destructive hailstorm and ruins the crop.
Hope: Lencho decides to ask God for help.
Post Office: The postmaster reads the letter, admires Lencho’s faith and arranges money.
Ending: Lencho receives less than he requested and writes again, believing that the postal employees are responsible for the missing amount.

Characters

Lencho: a hardworking farmer with extraordinary faith in God.
Postmaster: sympathetic and willing to help Lencho preserve his faith.
Lencho’s family: shares the loss caused by the storm.

Important Glossary

crest — top of a hill; dotted with — scattered over an area; hailstones — balls of ice falling with rain; locusts — insects that destroy crops; solitary — lonely; amiable — friendly; correspondence — exchange of letters; resolution — firm decision; contentment — satisfaction.

Textbook Focus

1. Where did Lencho live?
2. What did he hope for?
3. Why did he call the raindrops new coins?
4. What happened to the cornfield?
5. Who did Lencho have faith in?
6. Who read his letter?
7. What did the postmaster do?
8. Why was Lencho not surprised to receive money?

Central Ideas

Faith, hope after disaster, kindness, charity, irony and the difference between intention and perception.

Revision Points

Remember the sequence: expected rain → hailstorm → destroyed crop → letter to God → postmaster’s help → Lencho’s second letter.
