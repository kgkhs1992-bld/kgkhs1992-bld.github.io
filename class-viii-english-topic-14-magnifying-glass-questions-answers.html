Topic 14: Magnifying Glass

Questions & Answers

Poem – Short Answers

1. What is the central idea of the poem?

The poem shows how a magnifying glass reveals hidden details and makes small natural objects appear large, complex and wonderful.

2. Why is the magnifying glass described as magical?

It seems magical because it transforms the way ordinary objects are seen and reveals details that are normally too small to notice.

3. What happens to the small patch of moss when viewed through the glass?

It is imagined as a forest filled with flowers and trees, showing the imaginative power of magnification.

4. Why is the spider important in the poem?

The spider demonstrates how close observation reveals fascinating details such as its silk web, spinnerets, claws and eyes.

5. Why does the poet mention the moon at the end?

The moon extends the idea of lenses from tiny nearby objects to a distant celestial object, suggesting that lenses can make distant things seem closer.

6. What attitude does the speaker show towards nature?

The speaker shows fascination, curiosity and appreciation for the hidden details of the natural world.

Poetic Devices and Interpretation

7. Give an example of simile from the poem.

The drop of water is compared to a hive of bees using “like”.

8. What is personification in the phrase “Magic talk”?

The magnifying glass is treated as if it can perform or communicate magic, giving it a human-like quality.

9. What is visual imagery?

Visual imagery uses words to create pictures in the reader’s mind. The poem creates images of moss, forests, flowers, trees, spiders, webs and the moon.

10. Why are exclamation marks used in the poem?

They emphasise the speaker’s excitement, surprise and wonder while observing the magnified details.

It can be added to suitable nouns to form adjectives, as in tiger → tigerish.

Visual Aids and Astronomy

14. Name four visual aids mentioned in the lesson.

Magnifying spectacles, stand magnifiers, hand-held magnifiers and telescopes.

15. What is a stand magnifier?

It is a magnifier that rests above the object and helps keep the lens at a suitable distance, allowing hands-free use.

16. What are telescopes used for?

They are used to see distant objects or signs. The lesson also connects telescopes with the observation of celestial objects.

17. Where is the Indian Astronomical Observatory mentioned in the lesson?

It is described as a high-altitude astronomy station at Hanle, Ladakh.

18. What does the lesson say about the James Webb Space Telescope?

It describes it as a very large and powerful space telescope launched by NASA in 2021 and orbiting the Sun far beyond Earth.

Ancient and Modern Indian Scholars

19. Who was Aryabhata?

The lesson presents Aryabhata as an eminent ancient Indian astronomer whose work Aryabhatiyam was important for later astronomical development. It also mentions his heliocentric model and explanations of eclipses.

20. What is Panchasiddhantika?

It is the astronomical treatise associated in the lesson with Varahamihira, containing a compendium related to Greek, Egyptian, Roman and Indian astronomy.

21. What works of Bhaskaracharya II are mentioned?

Siddhantasiromani and Karanakutuhala are mentioned.

22. Why is Dr. Kailash Chandra Sahoo called “the planet hunter” in the lesson?

The lesson uses this popular description for his astronomical work involving exoplanets and other objects and phenomena in astronomy.

23. What areas of research are listed for Dr. Sahoo?

The lesson lists work involving nearby stars, stellar-mass black holes, exoplanets and different stellar populations of the Galactic bulge.

Long Answer Practice

24. Explain how the poem connects science with imagination.

The poem starts with a scientific instrument—the magnifying glass—but describes its effect through imaginative comparisons. Tiny natural objects become forests, hives and other large scenes. The poem therefore joins observation through a lens with the imagination of the observer.

25. How does Topic 14 connect a simple magnifying glass with astronomy?

The poem begins with close observation of tiny objects. The final image of the moon broadens the idea of lenses to distant objects. The lesson then discusses visual aids, telescopes, observatories and Indian astronomy, linking everyday observation with scientific study of space.
