The lesson explains air pollution, its sources, effects and control. It begins with the Bhopal industrial disaster as a warning and explains the importance of clean air. Industrial smoke, automobiles and harmful gases alter the normal composition of air. The lesson discusses health effects, global warming, damage to plants and buildings, acid rain and measures such as prevention, dispersal and collection of pollutants.

What is Air Pollution?

The textbook explains that air is normally a mixture of gases. When its natural composition is altered by reduced oxygen or irritating and harmful gases, the air becomes polluted.

Major Sources

Industrial activities release smoke, ash and toxic fumes. The lesson identifies thermal power stations and industries such as cement, steel, ore-processing and chemical industries as important sources. Automobile exhaust is another major source.

Effects on Health

Air pollution can cause eye irritation, scratchy throats and respiratory illnesses and is associated in the lesson with other serious diseases.

Environmental Damage

The lesson discusses damage to crops, trees, animals and buildings. It also explains how increasing greenhouse gases can contribute to global warming.

Acid Rain

Acid rain contains sulphur and nitrogen oxides and other chemicals. It can acidify lakes and rivers, harm aquatic life, remove nutrients from soil, damage forests and affect buildings and monuments.

Control Measures

Three basic approaches are described: preventive measures such as changing raw materials or fuel; dispersal measures such as raising smokestacks; and collection measures that trap pollutants before they escape.

Important Glossary

pollutant — harmful substance; respiratory — related to breathing; toxic — poisonous; acid rain — precipitation containing acidic pollutants; legislation — body of laws; smokestack — tall industrial chimney; norms — standards or rules.

Textbook Focus
