📚 Study Material

📚 7.1 Introduction

Trigonometry studies relationships between angles and sides of triangles. The textbook introduces its use in height-and-distance problems and astronomy, and mentions the historical development of the subject.

📐 7.2 Trigonometrical Ratios

For a right-angled triangle, with respect to an acute angle θ:

sin θ = perpendicular / hypotenuse
cos θ = base / hypotenuse
tan θ = perpendicular / base
cot θ = base / perpendicular
sec θ = hypotenuse / base
cosec θ = hypotenuse / perpendicular

The six ratios are related through reciprocal and quotient relations.

🔁 7.3 Relations among Trigonometrical Ratios

Reciprocal: sinθ·cosecθ=1, cosθ·secθ=1, tanθ·cotθ=1.
Square: sin²θ+cos²θ=1; sec²θ−tan²θ=1; cosec²θ−cot²θ=1.
Quotient: tanθ=sinθ/cosθ; cotθ=cosθ/sinθ.
Pythagoras relation: in a right triangle, p²+b²=h².

🧠 7.4 Simple Trigonometrical Identities

Important identities used in the textbook include:

sinθ·cosecθ = 1
cosθ·secθ = 1
tanθ·cotθ = 1
sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
sec²θ − tan²θ = 1
cosec²θ − cot²θ = 1
When proving an identity, simplify one side carefully using the basic identities until it becomes the other side.

⚡ Quick Revision

  • Remember the six definitions first.
  • Use reciprocal relations to change sin↔cosec, cos↔sec, tan↔cot.
  • Use sin²θ+cos²θ=1 for square relations.
  • Use tanθ=sinθ/cosθ and cotθ=cosθ/sinθ.
  • Memorise the 30°, 45°, 60° table.
  • For a right triangle, apply Pythagoras before finding a missing ratio.
