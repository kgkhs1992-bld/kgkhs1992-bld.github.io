📖 Chapter at a Glance

Author / Poet: Rabindranath Tagore
Chapter: The Flower-School
Textbook page: 67

The poem imagines flowers as children who attend a school underground. During their lessons the doors remain shut and the master keeps them disciplined. When the monsoon arrives, the flower children get their holidays and burst out in colourful dresses, dancing on the grass. The sky is imagined as their home, and the stars as their motherly world.

About the Poet

Rabindranath Tagore (1861–1941) was a poet, writer, painter, philosopher and educationist. The textbook associates nature strongly with his writing and notes that he received the Nobel Prize for Literature in 1913 for Gitanjali.

Stanza-wise Study

Opening: Storm clouds, June showers and the moist east wind announce the rain.
Flower-school: The speaker imagines flowers studying underground with doors shut.
Holiday: With the coming of rain, the flowers come out in colourful dresses and dance on the grass.
Home: The sky is imagined as their home, with the stars connected to their mother.

Personification

Flowers, clouds, wind, sky and other natural elements are presented as if they were people. This personification creates a playful picture of nature as a school and the flowers as schoolchildren.

Important Glossary

rumble — loud resonating sound; heath — large open area; moist — slightly wet; glee — happiness; underground — below the earth; bagpipes — wind instrument; advent — arrival.

Textbook Focus

1. What is the poem about?
2. What rumbles in the sky?
3. What comes marching over the heath?
4. Why does the speaker imagine an underground school?
5. What do the flowers do during their holidays?
6. What does the sky represent in the poem?

Central Ideas

Nature, imagination, childhood, the monsoon and the affectionate relationship between children and their mother.

Revision Points

Remember the personification, underground school, strict lessons, monsoon holidays, colourful flowers and the sky as home.
