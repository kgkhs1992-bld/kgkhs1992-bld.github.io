Chapter 02 – The Swimmer Who Does Not Need Her Legs!

KGK HIGH SCHOOL BELDANDIA

Study Material • Class IX English • Jasmine

1. Chapter Overview

The Swimmer Who Does Not Need Her Legs! tells the story of C.N. Janaki, a young swimmer who was struck by polio but trained to swim in the English Channel. Her physical disability did not stop her from working towards her goal. The chapter presents her training, the Channel attempt and the ideas she expressed after her success.

Janaki got the idea of swimming the English Channel as a child. The English Channel Swimming Association required a swimmer to be able to remain in water for at least ten hours continuously and to do marathon swimming in the high seas. Janaki trained hard in Bangalore for almost four years.

With the help of the Thimaiah National Academy of Adventure, she trained at Malpe in the Arabian Sea with coach Dinesh Suvarna. She swam a minimum of 20 kilometres a day. After receiving permission for the attempt, she and her parents went to England in the middle of 1992.

At Dover, Janaki trained under Channel coach Stella Streeter. A solo attempt was not arranged because a special boat was unavailable, so she became part of a six-member relay team. The team was named Janaki's Maritime Express in her honour.

The attempt took place on 28 July 1992. The cold and salty water, waves, seaweeds and jellyfish made the swim difficult. Janaki swam for about two hours and helped the relay team cross the 36 km Channel in 14 hours and 45 minutes.

Afterwards Janaki said that she wanted to prove that being handicapped was no bar to success. She emphasized hard work, determination, courage and self-reliance, and said there was no short-cut to success.

2. Important Facts

Person: C.N. Janaki
Illness: Polio, which made her legs useless for walking.
Idea: To swim the English Channel.
Training: Almost four years in Bangalore, followed by training at Malpe in the Arabian Sea.
Malpe coach: Dinesh Suvarna.
NIS coach/certificate: Mr. Gopal.
Channel coach: Stella Streeter.
Attempt: 28 July 1992.
Relay team: Six swimmers; Janaki was one of them.
Team name: Janaki's Maritime Express.
Channel distance and time: 36 km in 14 hours 45 minutes.
Janaki's share: About two hours of swimming.

3. Conditions for the Channel Attempt

  1. The swimmer should be able to spend at least ten hours continuously in water.
  2. The swimmer should be able to do marathon swimming in the high seas for long hours at a stretch.

4. Difficulties Faced During the Swim

  • The Channel water was very cold.
  • Janaki needed extra energy to keep her arms moving.
  • Her legs moved left or right with the waves.
  • The water was very salty.
  • Seaweeds and jellyfish stuck to her body and made her uncomfortable.

6. Language Focus

The chapter teaches the difference between could and be able to. “Could” can describe general ability, while “was/were able to” can describe successful performance in a particular situation.

Example: He could beat anybody. = He had the ability.
Example: He was able to beat him. = He managed to do it in that particular game.
