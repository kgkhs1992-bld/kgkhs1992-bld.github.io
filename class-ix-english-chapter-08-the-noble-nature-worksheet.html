Worksheet – 50 × 1 Mark

Chapter 08: The Noble Nature • Class IX English

1. Who is the poet?
Answer: Ben Jonson.
2. Which literary period is Ben Jonson associated with?
Answer: The English Renaissance.
3. What years are given for Ben Jonson?
Answer: 1572–1637.
4. Name the two plants/trees used in the comparison.
Answer: Oak and lily.
5. Which tree lives for hundreds of years?
Answer: Oak.
6. What kind of wood does an oak have?
Answer: Tough, hard wood.
7. What does sere mean?
Answer: Dried and withered.
8. What does proportions mean?
Answer: Quantities or relative sizes.
9. What does measures mean?
Answer: Units used to express size or extent.
10. Which flower is short-lived?
Answer: Lily.
11. Which is presented as fairer?
Answer: The lily.
12. Why is the lily beautiful?
Answer: It is beautiful despite its brief life.
13. What happens to the oak at the end?
Answer: It falls and becomes dry and withered.
14. What does the oak symbolize?
Answer: Long life, size and strength.
15. What does the lily symbolize?
Answer: Brief but beautiful life.
16. Does the poet make long life the only measure of worth?
Answer: No.
17. Can a short life be perfect?
Answer: Yes.
18. What does short measures suggest?
Answer: A short life may still be complete or perfect.
19. What does small proportions suggest?
Answer: Something small can still have beauty and value.
20. What is a major contrast in the poem?
Answer: The long-lived oak and short-lived lily.
21. What question about human life is raised?
Answer: Whether long life and prosperity alone make a person better.
22. What does the poem encourage us to value?
Answer: Quality and meaningfulness of life.
23. What does the title make us think about?
Answer: A noble or admirable person.
24. What does noble mean here?
Answer: Having admirable qualities.
25. Is the poem only about plants?
Answer: No, it uses plants to discuss human values.
26. Why is the oak a useful comparison?
Answer: Its size and long life contrast with the lily.
27. Why is the lily useful?
Answer: Its brief life shows duration is not the only measure of value.
28. What does the poem suggest about external greatness?
Answer: Size or duration alone does not guarantee worth.
29. What does the lily's beauty demonstrate?
Answer: A short existence can still be valuable.
30. What does the oak's final condition demonstrate?
Answer: Long duration does not prevent eventual decline.
31. What is the poem's philosophical idea?
Answer: Life should be judged by quality rather than length alone.
32. What is man compared to?
Answer: A sturdy oak and a delicate lily.
33. What professions are given for Ben Jonson?
Answer: Dramatist, poet and actor.
34. Was Ben Jonson a contemporary of Shakespeare?
Answer: Yes.
35. What is he best known for in the textbook?
Answer: Comedy of humour with a tone of satire.
36. What informed Jonson's poetry?
Answer: His classical learning.
37. What does the oak's long existence fail to provide permanently?
Answer: Lasting physical beauty.
38. What does the lily possess despite short life?
Answer: Beauty.
39. What does flower of Light emphasize?
Answer: Brightness and beauty.
40. What does the poem say about length of life?
Answer: Length alone does not determine worth.
41. What should students learn from the comparison?
Answer: Not to judge worth only by size or duration.
42. What poetic technique is prominent?
Answer: Contrast.
43. Which represents endurance over a long period?
Answer: The oak.
44. Which represents beauty in a short period?
Answer: The lily.
45. What does three hundred year emphasize?
Answer: The great length of the oak's life.
46. What does a lily of a day emphasize?
Answer: The lily's very brief life.
47. What is the relation between short life and beauty?
Answer: A short life can still be beautiful.
48. What should not be the only yardstick of worth?
Answer: Longevity.
49. What should not be the only sign of greatness?
Answer: Size or material prosperity.
50. Give the simplest message of the poem.
Answer: Meaningful quality matters more than mere length of life.
