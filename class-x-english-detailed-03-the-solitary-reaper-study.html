📖 Chapter at a Glance

Author / Poet: William Wordsworth
Chapter: The Solitary Reaper
Textbook page: 19

The poet sees a solitary Highland girl reaping grain and singing alone. He cannot understand the words, but the melody deeply affects him. He compares her song with the nightingale’s welcome notes and the cuckoo’s thrilling voice. He imagines that her song may express sorrow, loss, old memories or ancient battles. Even after he leaves, the music remains in his heart.

About the Poet

William Wordsworth (1770–1850) is presented in the textbook as a great poet of nature. He wrote about ordinary people and natural life and is associated with the Romantic age.

Scene and Characters

The scene is a Highland valley. The solitary reaper is a young farm worker cutting and binding grain while singing. The poet becomes a listener and observer.

Stanza-wise Study

Stanzas 1–2: The poet asks passers-by to notice the solitary Highland lass and describes her singing while she reaps.
Stanzas 3–4: Her song is compared with the nightingale and cuckoo because of its powerful effect.
Stanzas 5–7: The poet cannot understand the words and imagines possible subjects such as old sorrow, distant events and battles.
Stanza 8: The poet carries the music in his heart as he walks up the hill.

Important Glossary

solitary — alone; Highland — high mountain region; lass — girl; reaping — cutting grain; plaintive — expressing sadness; melancholy — sadness; Hebrides — group of islands off Scotland; chaunt — sing.

Textbook Focus

1. What is the central idea of the poem?
2. Who does the poet compare the reaper with?
3. Why can’t the poet understand the song?
4. What possibilities does he imagine about the song?
5. How does the song affect him?

Poetic Features

The poem uses imagery, comparison and sound. The nightingale and cuckoo are used as points of comparison. The unknown language makes the song mysterious while its melody remains universally moving.

Revision Points

Remember: Highland girl → reaping and singing → nightingale/cuckoo comparison → unknown subject → lasting memory.
