The chapter includes tangent segments, the radius–tangent right-angle theorem, equal tangent segments, the tangent–secant relation and common tangents.

Example 1 – Tangent and Radius

PT is tangent to a circle at T and OT is the radius. Find ∠OTP.

Solution:
A tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius drawn through the point of contact.
OT ⟂ PT
Therefore, ∠OTP = 90°.

Example 2 – Find the Other Tangent

From an external point P, PA and PB are two tangent segments to a circle. If PA = 8 cm, find PB.

Solution:
Tangent segments drawn from the same external point to a circle are equal.
PA = PB
So, PB = 8 cm.

Example 3 – Tangent–Secant Relation

From external point P, PA and PB are parts of a secant and PT is a tangent. If PA = 4 cm and PB = 9 cm, find PT.

Solution:
PA × PB = PT²
4 × 9 = PT²
PT² = 36
Therefore, PT = 6 cm.

Example 4 – Find PB

PA = 5 cm and PT = 10 cm. Find PB.

Solution:
PA × PB = PT²
5 × PB = 10² = 100
PB = 100 ÷ 5
Therefore, PB = 20 cm.

Example 5 – Another Tangent–Secant Problem

PA = 6 cm and PB = 24 cm. Find the tangent PT.

Solution: PT² = PA × PB = 6 × 24 = 144
PT = √144
Therefore, PT = 12 cm.

Example 6 – Two Equal Tangents

PA and PB are tangents from P. If PB = 15 cm, find PA.

Solution: Since both are tangent segments from the same external point P,
PA = PB
Hence, PA = 15 cm.

Example 7 – Right Triangle from a Tangent

O is the centre and PT is tangent at T. If OT = 7 cm and OP = 25 cm, find PT.

Solution:
OT ⟂ PT, so △OPT is right-angled at T.
By Pythagoras theorem:
OP² = OT² + PT²
25² = 7² + PT²
625 = 49 + PT²
PT² = 576
Therefore, PT = 24 cm.

Example 8 – Find the Radius

OP = 13 cm and tangent PT = 12 cm. Find OT.

Solution:
△OPT is right-angled at T.
OP² = OT² + PT²
13² = OT² + 12²
169 = OT² + 144
OT² = 25
Therefore, OT = 5 cm.

Example 9 – Tangent–Secant Calculation

PT = 15 cm and PA = 9 cm. Find PB.

Solution:
PA × PB = PT²
9 × PB = 15² = 225
PB = 225 ÷ 9
Therefore, PB = 25 cm.

Example 10 – Identify the Required Angle

A tangent touches a circle at T. The radius OT is drawn. What is the angle between OT and the tangent?

Solution:
Radius through the point of contact is perpendicular to the tangent.
∠OTP = 90°
Therefore, the required angle is 90°.

Example 11 – Pythagorean Application

OP = 17 cm and OT = 8 cm, where PT is tangent at T. Find PT.

Solution:
OP² = OT² + PT²
17² = 8² + PT²
289 = 64 + PT²
PT² = 225
Therefore, PT = 15 cm.

Example 12 – Find the Secant Length

PA = 12 cm and PT = 24 cm. Find PB.

Solution: 12 × PB = 24² = 576
PB = 576 ÷ 12
Therefore, PB = 48 cm.

Example 13 – Check Whether a Given Tangent Length is Correct

PA = 4 cm, PB = 16 cm and PT = 8 cm. Verify the tangent–secant relation.

Solution:
LHS = PA × PB = 4 × 16 = 64
RHS = PT² = 8² = 64
Since LHS = RHS, the relation is verified.

Example 14 – Two Tangents and Radius

PA and PB are tangent segments from P. If PA = 10 cm and OA is a radius, what is ∠OAP?

Solution:
OA is the radius through the point of contact A and PA is tangent.
Therefore, OA ⟂ PA.
Hence, ∠OAP = 90°.

Example 15 – Combined Application

OP = 10 cm, radius OT = 6 cm and PT is tangent at T. Find PT.

Solution:
OT ⟂ PT, so △OPT is right-angled at T.
OP² = OT² + PT²
10² = 6² + PT²
100 = 36 + PT²
PT² = 64
Therefore, PT = 8 cm.
🎯 Exam writing tip: For a 5-mark problem, always write the relevant theorem first, substitute the values clearly, show the square-root step when needed, and finish with the unit. For a proof, write the given information, construction/relations, theorem used, and conclusion separately.
Quick Formula Box
1. Radius through point of contact ⟂ tangent.
2. Tangent segments from the same external point are equal: PA = PB.
3. Tangent–secant relation: PA × PB = PT².
4. In the right triangle formed by centre, external point and point of contact: OP² = OT² + PT².
