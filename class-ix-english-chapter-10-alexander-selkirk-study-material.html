Chapter Overview

Alexander Selkirk is a poem by William Cowper. It presents the thoughts of a lonely person living on an isolated island. He has control over the land but deeply misses human companionship and his native country.

About the Poet

William Cowper (1731–1800) was an English poet. The textbook explains that the poem contains reflections associated with Alexander Selkirk and presents the emotional experience of loneliness.

Central Ideas

Solitude: Selkirk is alone and separated from other human beings.
Freedom and loneliness: He is the “monarch” of his surroundings, yet his freedom does not remove his loneliness.
Homesickness: His thoughts repeatedly return to his native land and friends.
Nature: Wind, sea birds and animals surround him, but they cannot replace human companionship.

Glossary

monarch – supreme ruler; brute – animal; solitude – loneliness; bestow – give; assuage – lessen suffering; desolate – isolated or barren; affliction – suffering; reconcile – bring into harmony.
