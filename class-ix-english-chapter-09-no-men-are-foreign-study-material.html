Chapter Overview

No Men Are Foreign by James Kirkup presents the idea of the essential unity of human beings. People may differ in country, language, clothing and boundaries, but all share the same basic human needs and experiences.

About the Poet

James Kirkup (1918–2009) was a poet, translator and travel writer. The textbook notes that he worked as a Professor of English and received the Atlantic Award for Literature in 1950.

Main Ideas

All people breathe, work, feel and live under the same sun, air and water.
Hatred and war harm humanity itself.
The repeated word Remember urges readers not to forget human unity.
The poem rejects the idea that people of other countries are naturally strange or foreign.

Key Vocabulary

foreign – belonging to another country; strange – unfamiliar; defile – make dirty or impure; outrage – shock or offend; dispossess – deprive someone of possession; betray – act disloyally.
