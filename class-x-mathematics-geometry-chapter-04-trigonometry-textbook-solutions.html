⭐ Part C – Textbook Simplification

1. cosec31° − sec59°

sec59° = cosec31°. Therefore the value is 0.

2. sin(50°+θ) − cos(40°−θ)

cos(40°−θ) = sin(50°+θ). Therefore the value is 0.

3. [cos²20° + cos²70°] / [sin²59° + sin²31°]

cos70° = sin20°, hence numerator = sin²20°+cos²20°=1.
Also sin59°=cos31°, hence denominator = cos²31°+sin²31°=1.
Therefore the value = 1.

4. tan(55°−θ) − cot(35°+θ)

cot(35°+θ)=tan[90°−(35°+θ)]=tan(55°−θ).
Therefore the value = 0.

5. cos²(90°+α) + cos²(180°−α)

cos(90°+α)=−sinα and cos(180°−α)=−cosα.
Therefore sin²α+cos²α = 1.

6. sec²(105°+α) − tan²(75°−α)

Let x=15°+α. Then sec(90°+x)=cosec x and tan(90°−x)=cot x.
So expression = cosec²x−cot²x = 1.

7. sin²(110°+α) + cos²(70°−α)

Let x=20°+α. Then sin(90°+x)=cos x and cos(90°−x)=sin x.
Hence expression = cos²x+sin²x = 1.

🧠 Part D – Textbook Proofs / Identities

1. Prove: cos(90°−θ) = sinθ.

In a right triangle, cos(90°−θ) = adjacent/hypotenuse with respect to the complementary angle. The corresponding side is the opposite side for θ. Hence cos(90°−θ) = sinθ.

2. Prove: sin(180°−θ) = sinθ.

From the associated-angle relation in the textbook, the angle 180°−θ lies in the second quadrant. Its perpendicular/opposite component has the same positive magnitude as for θ. Therefore sin(180°−θ)=sinθ.

3. Prove: cos(180°−θ) = −cosθ.

For 180°−θ, the cosine component lies on the negative x-direction. Thus its magnitude is equal to cosθ but its sign is negative: cos(180°−θ)=−cosθ.

4. Prove: tan(180°−θ) = −tanθ.

tan(180°−θ)=sin(180°−θ)/cos(180°−θ)=sinθ/(−cosθ)=−tanθ.

5. If A and B are acute angles and sinA = cosB, prove A+B=90°.

cosB = sin(90°−B). Hence sinA = sin(90°−B). Since A and 90°−B are both acute, A=90°−B. Therefore A+B=90°.

6. In a triangle ABC, B=90°. Prove sin²A + sin²C = 1.

Since A+C=90°, sinC=cosA. Therefore sin²A+sin²C = sin²A+cos²A = 1.

7. If A+B+C=180°, prove tanA+tanB+tanC=tanA·tanB·tanC.

Since A+B=180°−C, tan(A+B)=−tanC.
Using tan(A+B)=(tanA+tanB)/(1−tanA tanB):
(tanA+tanB)/(1−tanA tanB)=−tanC.
Therefore tanA+tanB+tanC = tanA·tanB·tanC.

📐 Part E – Compound Angles: Textbook-based Questions

1. Write the four compound-angle formulae.

sin(A+B)=sinA cosB+cosA sinB
cos(A+B)=cosA cosB−sinA sinB
sin(A−B)=sinA cosB−cosA sinB
cos(A−B)=cosA cosB+sinA sinB

2. Derive tan(A+B).

tan(A+B)=sin(A+B)/cos(A+B). Substituting the two compound-angle formulae and dividing numerator and denominator by cosA cosB gives
tan(A+B)=(tanA+tanB)/(1−tanA tanB).

3. Derive tan(A−B).

Using tan(A−B)=sin(A−B)/cos(A−B) and the corresponding formulae,
tan(A−B)=(tanA−tanB)/(1+tanA tanB).

4. Prove: sin(A+B)+sin(A−B)=2sinA cosB.

Add the two formulae:
sinAcosB+cosAsinB + sinAcosB−cosAsinB = 2sinAcosB.

5. Prove: sin(A+B)−sin(A−B)=2cosA sinB.

Subtract the two formulae. The sinAcosB terms cancel and the remaining terms give 2cosA sinB.

6. Prove: cos(A+B)+cos(A−B)=2cosA cosB.

Adding the two cosine formulae gives 2cosAcosB because the ±sinA sinB terms cancel.

7. Prove: cos(A−B)−cos(A+B)=2sinA sinB.

Subtract cos(A+B) from cos(A−B):
(cosAcosB+sinAsinB)−(cosAcosB−sinAsinB)=2sinA sinB.

8. Find sin15°.

sin15°=sin(45°−30°)
=sin45°cos30°−cos45°sin30°
= (√2/2)(√3/2)−(√2/2)(1/2)
= (√6−√2)/4.

9. Find tan105°.

tan105°=tan(60°+45°)
=(√3+1)/(1−√3)=−(2+√3).

10. Prove: cos70°+cos50°=sin80°.

cos70°+cos50°=cos(60°+10°)+cos(60°−10°)
=2cos60°cos10°=cos10°=sin80°.
Hence proved.

11. Prove: tan70°·tan65°−tan70°−tan65°=1.

Since 70°+65°=135°, tan(70°+65°)=−1.
Thus (tan70°+tan65°)/(1−tan70°tan65°)=−1.
Rearranging gives tan70°tan65°−tan70°−tan65°=1.

12. If A+B+C=180°, prove tanA+tanB+tanC=tanA tanB tanC.

This follows by putting A+B=180°−C in the tan(A+B) formula. The final relation is tanA+tanB+tanC=tanA tanB tanC.

📊 Part F – Standard-angle Textbook Calculations

1. Evaluate: cos²135° − 2sin²180° + 3cot²150° − 4tan²120°.

cos²135°=1/2; sin180°=0; cot150°=−√3; tan120°=−√3.
Therefore = 1/2 + 0 + 3(3) − 4(3) = −5/2.

2. Evaluate: tan30°·tan135°·tan150°·tan45°.

=(1/√3)(−1)(−1/√3)(1)=1/3.

3. Evaluate: [sec²180°+tan150°]/[cosec²90°+cot120°].

Numerator = 1−1/√3. Denominator = 1−1/√3. Hence value = 1.

4. Evaluate: sin²135°+cos²120°−sin²120°+tan²150°.

=1/2+1/4−3/4+1/3 = 1/3.

5. Prove: tan1°·tan2°·tan3°···tan89°=1.

Pair tanθ with tan(90°−θ)=cotθ. Each pair has product 1, and the middle term tan45°=1. Therefore the complete product is 1.

6. Prove: cot12°·cot38°·cot52°·cot60°·cot78° = 1/√3.

cot78°=tan12° and cot52°=tan38°, so the first four paired factors give 1. Remaining cot60°=1/√3. Hence answer 1/√3.

7. Prove: tan5°·tan15°·tan45°·tan75°·tan85°=1.

tan85°=cot5°, tan75°=cot15°, tan45°=1. Therefore product = 1.

🏗️ Part G – Heights & Distances: Textbook Examples

1. A tower is observed from 75 m away. The angle of elevation of its top is 30°. Find its height.

Let height = h.
tan30° = h/75.
h=75/√3=25√3 ≈ 43.3 m (using √3=1.732).

2. A 30 m tall tree is observed from a point and the angle of elevation is 30°. Find the horizontal distance.

tan30°=30/d.
d=30/tan30°=30√3≈51.96 m.

3. From a point on the ground, the angle of elevation of a hill-top is 30° and the point is 100 m from the foot. If another line of sight gives 60° as shown in the textbook, find the height.

The textbook configuration gives two right triangles with the same height. Equating the corresponding tangent relations gives the height as 150 m, as shown in the worked textbook example.

4. Two angles of elevation of the top of a tower are 45° and 30°, and the observation points are 30 m apart. Find the tower height.

Let tower height = x. For 45°, the nearer horizontal distance = x. For 30°, the farther distance = x√3. Their difference is 30 m:
x√3−x=30.
x=30/(√3−1)=15(√3+1)≈40.98 m.

5. A tower is 100 m away from an observation line. The angles of elevation from two points are 30° and 60° as in the textbook figure. Find the tower height.

Using the two tangent equations from the textbook configuration:
tan30°=(x−100)/QD and tan60°=x/QD.
Eliminating QD gives x=150. Hence the tower height is 150 m.

6. A point is 12 m from a tower and the angle of elevation of the top is 60°. What relation gives the tower height?

If the horizontal distance is 12 m, then tan60°=h/12. Hence h=12√3 m.

7. A 2 m tall observer sees the top of a 24 m tower at an angle of elevation of 30°. Find the horizontal distance.

Vertical difference = 24−2=22 m.
tan30°=22/d.
d=22√3≈38.1 m.

8. A tree is 30 m high and the angle of elevation of the top from a point is 60°. Find the horizontal distance.

tan60°=30/d ⇒ d=30/√3=10√3 m ≈17.32 m.
