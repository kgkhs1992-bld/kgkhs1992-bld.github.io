⭐ Additional Questions & Answers

Extra practice questions prepared from the chapter content.

Q1. Why is the poem presented as a conversation?
Answer: The dialogue makes the different desires of mother and daughter clear.
Q2. What does “honey child” suggest?
Answer: It is an affectionate way for the mother to address her daughter.
Q3. Why are bridal images important?
Answer: They represent the mother’s expectations about marriage and domestic life.
Q4. Why does the daughter prefer forest notes?
Answer: She experiences them as freer and sweeter than the sounds of worldly life.
Q5. How does repetition affect the poem?
Answer: Repeated phrases give it the rhythm and quality of folk song.
Q6. What does the forest symbolize in the poem’s imagery?
Answer: It represents nature, freedom and the daughter’s longing.
Q7. How does the poem use sound imagery?
Answer: It refers to the koil, forest notes, bridal songs and cradle songs.
Q8. What does the ending show?
Answer: The daughter remains determined to follow the call of the forest.
Q9. How does the poem show two generations or viewpoints?
Answer: The mother emphasizes marriage and social life, while the daughter follows her attraction to nature.
Q10. Why is the title suitable?
Answer: The poem has the simple, musical quality and rural setting associated with a village song.
