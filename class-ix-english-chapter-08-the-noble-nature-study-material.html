Chapter Overview

The Noble Nature is a poem by Ben Jonson (1572–1637). It asks what makes a human life noble and valuable. The poet contrasts the long life of an oak with the brief but beautiful life of a lily.

The poem's central idea is that size, length of life and material prosperity alone do not determine worth. A short life can still be beautiful, meaningful and complete.

About the Poet

Ben Jonson was an English Renaissance dramatist, poet and actor. He was a contemporary of William Shakespeare. The textbook notes his classical learning and his association with comedy and satire.

Oak and Lily – The Main Comparison

Oak: large, strong and long-lived; after its long existence it finally falls and becomes dry and withered.
Lily: short-lived but beautiful; its brief existence is presented as valuable and fair.
Human life: the poem asks readers to judge life by its quality and meaningfulness rather than by duration alone.

Key Themes

  • Quality over quantity
  • True nobility and human worth
  • Beauty can exist in a brief life
  • Long life is not the only measure of value
  • Meaningful living is more important than mere duration

Glossary
