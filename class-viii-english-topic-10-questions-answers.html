Questions & Answers

Class VIII English • Jasmine • Unit 4: Environment • The Cherry Tree

These answers are based on the textbook lesson and the reading-comprehension questions in the Class VIII Jasmine English textbook.

Part I — Think and Reflect

1. Why did Rakesh’s parents send him to Mussoorie?
Rakesh’s parents sent him to Mussoorie because there were no schools in their village. They wanted him to attend school, so they sent him to stay with his grandfather in Mussoorie.
2. Why did Rakesh let the praying-mantis remain on the branch of the cherry tree?
He let it remain because he did not want to disturb it. He regarded it as the cherry tree’s first visitor.
3. How is life in the Himalayan foothills described in the story?
The Himalayan foothills are shown as a mountainous place with stony soil and dry, cold winds that can stunt plant growth. There are sheltered slopes with oak and deodar forests, terraced fields, monsoon rains, snow and changing seasons. The natural surroundings are an important part of Rakesh’s life.
4. What might Rakesh mean when he says, “Is this what it feels to be God?” at the end of the story?
Rakesh is expressing his wonder at having planted a tiny seed and watched it grow into a large tree. He feels a sense of amazement and fulfilment at seeing something he helped create and nurture become part of the living world.
5. How does the writer describe the relationship between Rakesh and his grandfather?
Their relationship is warm and affectionate. Grandfather guides Rakesh, gives him practical advice and encourages him to care for the cherry tree. Rakesh shares his discoveries with Grandfather, and they enjoy the tree and the natural surroundings together.
6. What can you infer about the connection between humans and nature based on the story?
The story shows that people can develop a close connection with nature by planting, caring for and observing living things. The cherry tree becomes special to Rakesh and Grandfather because they planted it themselves and watched it grow.
7. Imagine Rakesh reflects on his wonderful experience of watching a seed become a full-grown cherry tree. How might he have felt?
He might have felt happy, proud, amazed and grateful. He had seen the seed survive difficult conditions and grow into a tree that gave shade, blossoms and fruit and attracted birds and insects.

Part II — Extract-based Questions

1. What does Rakesh’s action of rolling the cherry seed in his mouth most likely suggest?
It suggests that he was enjoying the taste of the cherry and the moment before examining the seed and deciding what to do with it.
2. What does Grandfather mean by saying, “Nothing is lucky if you put it away. If you want luck, you must put it to some use”?
Grandfather means that simply keeping something does not make it useful or valuable. Rakesh should put the seed to use by planting it.
3. What does the line “field-mice sought shelter in the roof of the cottage” suggest?
It shows that winter was severe and cold, so the field-mice looked for a warm, protected place inside the cottage.
4. What can be inferred about the cherry tree’s toughness from the line “The cherry tree bent low with the weight of snow”?
The tree could withstand harsh weather. Although the snow bent it down, it continued to grow and later produced blossoms.
5. How did the early winter affect Grandfather’s mood and storytelling?
The cold and blocked road made Grandfather grumpy. His stories also began to have unhappy endings.
6. What does the comparison of Rakesh’s age with the tree’s age suggest?
It highlights the tree’s rapid growth alongside Rakesh’s own growth. By the time Rakesh was nine, the four-year-old tree was almost as tall as he was.

Quick Answer Key

  1. There was no school in Rakesh’s village, so his parents sent him to Mussoorie.
  2. He allowed the praying-mantis to stay because it was the tree’s first visitor.
  3. The Himalayan foothills are shown through mountains, forests, terraced fields, cold winds, rain and snow.
  4. Rakesh feels wonder at having helped a tiny seed become a tree.
  5. Rakesh and Grandfather share a close, caring relationship.
  6. The story presents planting and caring for a tree as a way of forming a bond with nature.
  7. Rakesh would likely feel proud, happy, amazed and grateful.
