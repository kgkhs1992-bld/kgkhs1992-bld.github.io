Book Q & A – Chapter 08

Original KGK answers based on the Class IX textbook chapter.

1. Who wrote The Noble Nature?
Answer: Ben Jonson.
2. When did Ben Jonson live?
Answer: 1572–1637.
3. What two natural objects are compared?
Answer: An oak and a lily.
4. What does the oak represent?
Answer: Long life, size and strength.
5. What does the lily represent?
Answer: Brief but beautiful life.
6. What happens to the oak after its long life?
Answer: It falls and becomes dry and withered.
7. Why is the lily presented as fair?
Answer: Its brief life is associated with beauty.
8. Does long life automatically make a person better?
Answer: No.
9. What does sere mean?
Answer: Dried and withered.
10. What is the central message?
Answer: The quality and meaningfulness of life matter more than length alone.
11. Why is the oak used as a comparison?
Answer: Its great size and long life contrast with the lily.
12. Why is the lily used as a comparison?
Answer: Its brief life shows that duration is not the only measure of value.
13. What do short measures suggest?
Answer: A short life can still be complete or perfect.
14. What do small proportions suggest?
Answer: Something limited can still have beauty and value.
15. What qualities are connected with nobility?
Answer: Qualities that make a life admirable and meaningful.
16. Is physical size the main measure of worth?
Answer: No.
17. Is longevity the only measure of a good life?
Answer: No.
18. What contrast is central to the poem?
Answer: The long-lived oak and the short-lived lily.
19. What does the oak finally become?
Answer: A dry, bald and withered log.
20. What does the lily teach us?
Answer: A brief life can still contain beauty and value.
21. Why is the poem called The Noble Nature?
Answer: It reflects on qualities that make a person or life noble.
22. What literary method is prominent?
Answer: Comparison and contrast.
23. What does the poem make us question?
Answer: Whether long life or prosperity alone determine worth.
24. What is more important than duration?
Answer: Quality and meaningfulness of life.
25. What is the poet's attitude toward the lily?
Answer: He presents it as beautiful and valuable despite its short life.
26. What does the oak's age fail to guarantee?
Answer: Permanent beauty or physical existence.
27. What does the poem suggest about material prosperity?
Answer: It should not be the sole measure of human worth.
28. What does flower of Light emphasize?
Answer: The lily's brightness and beauty.
29. How should people be judged?
Answer: Not merely by size, duration or possessions.
30. What is the main lesson for students?
Answer: Live meaningfully and develop admirable qualities.
