The lesson introduces the cultural diversity of North-East India and explains how festivals are connected with agriculture, community life, music and dance. It discusses festivals across the region, including the Bihu festivals of Assam and celebrations in Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Sikkim and Tripura.

Regional Picture

The lesson presents North-East India as a culturally diverse region. The seven states commonly called the Seven Sisters are discussed along with Sikkim as the younger brother.

Assam — Bihu

Rongali/Bohag Bihu is linked with spring, the beginning of sowing and the Assamese New Year. Kongali/Kaati Bihu is observed in mid-October when the fields are green but barns are empty. Bhogali/Magh Bihu is observed in mid-January as a thanksgiving at the end of harvest.

Music and Dance

Bihu celebrations are closely associated with folk songs and traditional dance. The textbook mentions instruments such as cymbals, bamboo clappers, dhol and pepa.

Other Festivals

The lesson describes celebrations such as Losar and Mopin in Arunachal Pradesh, Ningol Chakouba and Cheiraoba in Manipur, Wangala in Meghalaya, Chapchar Kut in Mizoram, Moastu and Hornbill Festival in Nagaland, and festivals such as Saga Dawa and Losoong in Sikkim.
