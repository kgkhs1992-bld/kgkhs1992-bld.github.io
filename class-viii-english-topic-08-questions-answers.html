The Magic Brush of Dreams

Class VIII English • Jasmine • Questions & Answers

🤔 Let us Discuss — Page 127

Q. Arrange the events in order of occurrence in the poem.
Answer: 1. Gopi is told to gather food but instead sits drawing pictures in the sand.
2. A man gives a magic brush to Gopi to paint only for the poor.
3. Gopi paints a bowl of khichdi, which becomes real and amazes the villagers.
4. Gopi uses the magic brush to paint useful items for the villagers.
5. The Zamindar orders Gopi to paint treasures for him.
6. The Zamindar throws Gopi into prison.
7. Gopi paints a road and a horse to escape.
8. Gopi returns to the village with the Zamindar and his men chasing her.
9. Gopi paints a wide river, stopping the Zamindar and his men.
10. Gopi scares the Zamindar away, and celebrates with the villagers.
Q. Fill in the blanks: tone of the poem.
Answer: The tone of the poem shifts throughout. Initially, the tone is playful and innocent as Gopi uses her imagination to draw in the sand. When the Zamindar enters the story, the tone shifts to tension and suspense. In the final part, it becomes triumphant and celebratory as Gopi escapes, the Zamindar is defeated, and the villagers rejoice.

🤔 Let us Discuss — Page 128

Q. Fill in the blanks: rhyme scheme.
Answer: The poem mostly follows a simple rhyme scheme, ABCB, which adds a rhythmic flow.
Q. Which poetry genre does the poem belong to?
Answer: The poem belongs to the narrative or ballad poetry genre, which tells a story through verse.
Q. What is special about the brush in the poem?
Answer: The correct answer is (ii) It can bring painting to life.
Q. What does the poem ultimately teach us?
Answer: The correct answer is (ii) Power and kindness should be used to help the poor and needy.
Q. Match the phrases with what they symbolise.
Answer: The magic paintbrush → the power of art and imagination to change the world.
The grand fortress → greed and the desire for wealth.
The river and the beast → barriers to protect Gopi from the Zamindar's anger.
Q. True or False: Gopi uses the magic brush to help others, while the Zamindar wants to use it for personal gain.
Answer: True.

🧠 Let us Think and Reflect — Pages 128–130

Q. Extract 1: What does the man's whispering and secret instruction reflect?
Answer: It reflects his careful and secretive nature.
Q. What is the significance of the man slipping the brush into Gopi's hand?
Answer: It signifies that he is secretly passing on a great power and responsibility to Gopi, choosing her because he trusts her pure and kind heart.
Q. Why does the man instruct Gopi to paint only for the poor?
Answer: He knows the wealthy might misuse the magic for their own greed, while the poor truly need help to survive.
Q. What is the most appropriate title for Extract 1?
Answer: The Magic Brush's Secret.
Q. Why does the news of Gopi's magic brush spread through forests, fields, and plains?
Answer: Because of its miraculous ability to bring painted objects to life and solve poor people's problems.
Q. Why are the things painted by Gopi compared to 'magic gains'?
Answer: Because the paintings turn into real, physical objects instantly, providing magical benefits and wealth to those who receive them.
Q. Which statement is false: (A) Gopi's magic brush draws the attention of the Zamindar. (B) The Zamindar requests Gopi to paint a grand fortress and jewels.
Answer: Statement B is false. The Zamindar does not request Gopi; he greedily orders her to paint a grand fortress and jewels.
Q. What does the Zamindar's demand for a grand fortress and jewels reveal about his character?
Answer: It reveals that he is extremely greedy, power-hungry, and selfish, caring only about expanding his own wealth rather than helping his people.
Q. Why did Gopi make the right decision by refusing to paint for the Zamindar?
Answer: Gopi had promised the old man that she would use the brush only to help the poor, not to satisfy the greed of the wealthy. Painting for the Zamindar would have broken her promise and misused the magic.
Q. Justify Gopi's action in threatening to draw a beast. How would you deal with the situation differently?
Answer: Gopi's action is justified as a necessary act of self-defense against the Zamindar who was relentlessly chasing her. The supplied answer suggests painting a thick wall to block him permanently or a cage to trap him so he could face justice.
Q. What is the significance of Gopi celebrating with the villagers at the end?
Answer: The celebration signifies the triumph of good over evil. It shows that Gopi used the magic brush for its true purpose—bringing happiness, unity, and prosperity to the community.
Q. Compare Gopi's use of the magic brush with how the Zamindar would have used it.
Answer: Gopi used the magic brush selflessly to help others by painting food, tools, and clothing for poor villagers. In contrast, the Zamindar would have used it selfishly to paint fortresses, jewels, and gold to increase his own power and personal wealth.
Q. What can be inferred about Gopi's character from her refusal to follow the Zamindar's orders?
Answer: Gopi is courageous, principled, and loyal. She is not intimidated by power or wealth and has a strong sense of integrity.
Q. Justify the title 'The Magic Brush of Dreams'.
Answer: The title is appropriate because the central object is a magic brush that turns drawn objects into reality. It fulfills the dreams of poor villagers by providing food, tools, clothing, and joy.
Q. If you had the magic brush, how would you use it in today's world?
Answer: The supplied answer suggests using it to paint food and clean water for people suffering from hunger, plant thousands of trees, and create homes and hospitals for the poor and sick.
