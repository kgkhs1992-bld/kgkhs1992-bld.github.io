3. Prove

  1. In two similar triangles, the lengths of corresponding altitudes are proportional to the lengths of corresponding sides.
  2. In two similar triangles, the lengths of corresponding angle bisectors are proportional to the lengths of corresponding sides.
  3. In two similar triangles, the lengths of corresponding medians are proportional to the lengths of corresponding sides.
Solution: Draw the corresponding altitude/angle-bisector/median in each pair. The resulting smaller triangles are similar by AA similarity. Using corresponding-side ratios gives the required proportionality. Thus each corresponding special segment has the same scale factor as the corresponding side.

4. Equal perimeters of similar triangles

Two similar triangles have equal perimeters. Prove that the two triangles are congruent.

Proof: Let the common ratio of corresponding sides be k. Similarity gives the same ratio for perimeters, so k = P₂/P₁ = 1. Hence every pair of corresponding sides is equal. Therefore the two triangles are congruent by SSS.

5. Equal areas of similar triangles

Two similar triangles have equal areas. Prove that the two triangles are congruent.

Proof: For similar triangles, area ratio equals the square of the ratio of corresponding sides. Equal areas give 1 = k², so k = 1 (lengths are positive). Therefore all corresponding sides are equal and the triangles are congruent by SSS.

6. Area ratio and corresponding segments

Prove that the ratio of the areas of two similar triangles is equal to:

  1. the square of the ratio of corresponding altitudes;
  2. the square of the ratio of corresponding angle-bisectors;
  3. the square of the ratio of corresponding medians;
  4. the square of the ratio of their perimeters.
Answer: Since corresponding altitudes, angle-bisectors, medians and sides all have the same similarity scale factor k, and area ratio is k², each required area ratio is the square of the corresponding segment ratio.

7. Equal-area triangles in a triangle

Points P and Q lie on AB and AC respectively such that ΔBPQ and ΔCPQ have equal areas. Prove the required proportional relationship between the indicated segments in the textbook figure.

Solution: Use the common altitude from A/appropriate base-height pairs and equate the two areas. Cancel the common factors and apply the basic proportionality relation. This produces the proportional relationship stated in the textbook.

8. Intersecting lines and similar triangles

In Fig. 1.52, AB and CD intersect at O.

  1. If AO × OD = BO × OC, prove ΔAOC ~ ΔBOD.
  2. If CO × OD = AO × OB, prove ΔAOC ~ ΔDOB.
  3. State the condition under which AC and DB are parallel.
Solution: Use the vertically opposite angles at O together with the given product relation. The product condition can be rewritten as a proportion of corresponding sides; with the common angle at O, AA/SAS similarity follows. The converse gives the required parallel condition.

9. Trapezium area

ABCD is a trapezium with AB ∥ DC. Diagonals AC and BD intersect at O. AO = 3 cm, OC = 5 cm and area of ΔAOB is 36 cm². Find the area of ΔCOD.

Solution: ΔAOB ~ ΔCOD. Therefore corresponding side ratio = AO:OC = 3:5, so area ratio = 9:25.
Area(ΔCOD) = 36 × 25/9 = 100 cm².

10. Ratio of areas

In Fig. 1.53, ΔABC and ΔDBC have the same base BC and O is the intersection of AC and BD. Prove that

Area(ΔABD) / Area(ΔBCD) = AO / OC.
Proof: Use the similarity of the relevant triangles formed by the intersecting lines and the common-base/parallel relationships in the figure. Corresponding linear ratios give the stated area ratio.

11. Midpoint theorem construction

Prove that the line segments joining the midpoints of the sides of a triangle divide it into four triangles. Prove that these four triangles are congruent and each is similar to the original triangle. Also prove that each small triangle has one-fourth the area of the original triangle.

Proof: By the midpoint theorem, each joining segment is parallel to the third side and half its length. Each small triangle therefore has corresponding side ratio 1:2 with the original triangle, so it is similar to the original. All four small triangles have the same three side lengths and are congruent. Their area ratio is (1/2)² = 1/4.

12. Rectangle inside a right triangle

In Fig. 1.54, ∠ABC is a right angle and PQRS is a rectangle. Prove that ΔAPS ~ ΔQCR ~ ΔPQB ~ ΔACB.

Proof: Because PQRS is a rectangle, its sides are respectively parallel/perpendicular to the sides of the right triangle. The corresponding acute angles are equal and the right angles are equal. Hence each of the four triangles is similar to ΔACB by AA similarity.

13. Trapezium with parallel sides

In Fig. 1.55, AB ∥ DC. If ΔADO ~ ΔBCO, prove that AD = BC.

Proof: From the similarity, AD/BC equals AO/OC (corresponding sides). Since AB ∥ DC and the intersecting diagonals create the corresponding proportional segments, AO = OC in the stated configuration. Hence AD = BC.

14. Prove a geometric-mean relation

In trapezium ABCD, AD ∥ BC. If ∠ABD = ∠DCB, prove that BD² = AD × BC.

Proof: The angle condition together with AD ∥ BC gives a pair of equal corresponding angles, so the appropriate triangles are similar. Hence BD/AD = BC/BD. Cross multiplication gives BD² = AD × BC.

15. Midpoint theorem

In ΔABC, X and Y lie on AB and AC respectively such that XY ∥ BC. Prove that the median AD of ΔABC bisects XY.

Proof: Since XY ∥ BC, triangles AXY and ABC are similar. Therefore AX/AB = AY/AC. As AD is a median, AB = 2AD-side relation gives the midpoint correspondence; the line through A and the midpoint D meets the parallel segment XY at its midpoint. Hence AD bisects XY.

16. Median and a line through the vertex

In ΔABC, AD is a median and E is the midpoint of AD. BE meets AC at X. Prove that BE = 3EX.

Proof: Apply the midpoint theorem and the converse in the triangles formed by the median and the transversal BE. The relevant triangles are similar with scale factor 3:1, giving BE : EX = 3 : 1, hence BE = 3EX.

17. Right triangle from a geometric-mean condition

In ΔABC, AD ⟂ BC and AD² = DC × BD. Prove: (i) ∠BAC is a right angle; (ii) the areas of ΔABD and ΔCAD are proportional to AB² and AC².

Proof: The relation AD² = BD·DC is the converse of the altitude/geometric-mean theorem for a right triangle. Hence ∠BAC = 90°. The two smaller triangles are similar to the original triangle, so their areas follow the squares of the corresponding sides, giving the required proportionality.

18. Midpoints of corresponding sides

In ΔABC and ΔDEF, ∠A = ∠D and ∠B = ∠E. X and Y are the midpoints of BC and EF respectively. Prove (i) ΔAXC ~ ΔDYF and (ii) ΔAXB ~ ΔDYE.

Proof: Since ΔABC ~ ΔDEF by AA, corresponding sides and corresponding angles are proportional/equal. Because X and Y are midpoints, CX = BX = BC/2 and FY = EY = EF/2. Therefore the required pairs of triangles are similar by AA/SAS.

19. Geometric mean in Fig. 1.56

AB is the perpendicular Q-side configuration shown in Fig. 1.56; QR ∥ BC and DR ∥ QC, with A-R-C and A-D-B. Prove that AQ² = AD × AB.

Proof: The parallels create two pairs of equal corresponding angles, so the triangles containing AQ, AD and AB are similar. From corresponding-side proportionality, AQ/AD = AB/AQ. Therefore AQ² = AD × AB.

20. Three parallel lines

In Fig. 1.57, AB ∥ CD ∥ EF and AF and BE intersect at C. Prove that EF × BD = DF × AB.

Proof: The three parallel lines cut the two transversals proportionally. Thus EF/DF = AB/BD. Cross multiplication gives EF × BD = DF × AB.

21. Inradius and corresponding side ratio

For two similar triangles, prove that the ratio of their inradii is equal to the ratio of corresponding sides.

Proof: Similar triangles have the same shape and scale factor k. Every length obtained by a corresponding construction, including the perpendicular distance from the incenter to a side (the inradius), scales by k. Hence r₁/r₂ equals the corresponding side ratio.

22. Uniqueness of the midpoint

If A–P–B and A–Q–B and PB = QB, prove that P and Q are not distinct.

Proof: Since P and Q lie on the same segment AB, PB = QB means both points are at the same distance from B along the same line segment. Therefore they occupy the same position: P = Q.

23. Converse of the angle-bisector relation

In Fig. 1.58, ∠ABC is obtuse and AD ⟂ BC. If AD² = BD × DC, prove that ∠BAD and ∠CAD are in the required corresponding relation.

Proof: The condition AD² = BD·DC gives the right-triangle similarity condition in the converse direction. Hence the two triangles formed by AD have the required similarity, from which the stated angle relation follows.

24. Area of a trapezium and a triangle

X and Y lie on AB and AC respectively with XY ∥ BC. If the area of trapezium XBCY is eight times the area of ΔAXY, find AX : BX.

Solution: Let area(ΔAXY) = S. Then area(ABC) = S + 8S = 9S. Since ΔAXY ~ ΔABC, the side ratio squared equals the area ratio:
(AX/AB)² = 1/9, so AX/AB = 1/3.
Therefore AX : XB = 1 : 2. Answer: 1:2.

25. Parallelogram and proportional segments

ABCD is a parallelogram. AG is drawn and meets BD, CD and BC at E, F and G respectively as shown in the textbook figure. Prove that AE : EG = AF : AG.

Proof: Use the parallel opposite sides of the parallelogram and apply the Basic Proportionality Theorem to the triangles cut by AG. The parallel lines produce equal corresponding angles, so the relevant triangles are similar. Hence the corresponding divisions on AG are proportional, giving AE : EG = AF : AG.
Exam tip: For every similarity proof, clearly write the angle equalities first, name the triangles in corresponding order, state the similarity criterion (AA/SAS/SSS), and then write the required proportional relation.
